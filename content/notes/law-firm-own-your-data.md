**The short version:** a small firm can get the benefits of modern data tools, like search, reporting, and AI, while keeping client data on infrastructure the firm owns. The setup lives in your own cloud account, access is limited to who needs it, and your managed IT provider or an outside expert can run it. Stricter security needs can be met with hosted options that don't have to be expensive.

## Why this matters more for law firms

Most modern software asks you to move your data into someone else's platform. For many businesses, that's a reasonable trade. For a law firm, client information is the most sensitive thing you hold, and confidentiality isn't optional.

That concern is one reason law firms tend to adopt technology more slowly. It's a good instinct. But it often means firms miss out on tools that would save real time, because the only options seem to require trusting another vendor with client data.

There's another way.

## Build on infrastructure you own

When I set up data tools for a firm, I build them on infrastructure the firm owns.

- **If you already have a cloud account,** like Microsoft Azure, it goes there. Many firms already have one through Microsoft 365.
- **If you don't,** I set one up in the firm's name, on Azure, AWS, or a similar provider, so the firm owns everything from day one.

The major cloud platforms are secure by default. The firm holds the keys, the data never sits on a vendor's servers, and if you ever part ways with whoever set it up, everything stays with you.

## Give people only the access they need

Owning the infrastructure is the start. The next step is controlling who can see what.

Not everyone gets the same password or admin access. Each person and each tool gets access to the specific data and reports they need, and nothing more. In a smaller firm with fewer people and less data, that's simple to set up. As a firm grows, adds compliance requirements, or handles more sensitive matters, access gets more specific, and the tools support that.

## Work with your IT provider

Many small firms rely on a managed IT provider, and owning your own technology can sound daunting. It doesn't have to change that relationship.

I can work alongside your IT provider to stand up the infrastructure, so it fits how they already support you and they stay in the loop. Some firms would rather have someone outside run these services for them, and that's an option too. But it isn't always needed. Set up well, this kind of setup mostly runs itself.

## When security requirements are stricter

Some firms, or some clients, need more than good defaults. They need contractual guarantees about how data is stored and moved.

There are solutions built for exactly that. Hosted databases like BigQuery, Snowflake, or MotherDuck (a hosted version of DuckDB), and data movement services like Fivetran, can check those boxes for storage and transfer. These options don't have to be expensive. For a small firm's data, some of them cost very little.

## What owning your data makes possible

Once your firm's data lives in one place you control, a lot opens up: [one search across Outlook, your files, and your case management software](/notes/law-firm-unified-search), [better intake without new software](/notes/law-firm-intake-without-new-software), reporting without exports, and AI that works on your own records instead of the open internet.

None of that requires handing your client data to someone else.

## If you only do one thing

Make a list of every place your firm's client data lives today, and who controls each one. The answer is often more vendors than anyone realized.
