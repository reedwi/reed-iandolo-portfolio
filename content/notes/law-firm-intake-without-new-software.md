**The short version:** you don't need a new intake platform to fix intake. Most small firms can streamline it inside the tools they already use, like Outlook and their shared files, with a small amount of custom software that captures details once, files them in the right place, and kicks off the next step. The firm keeps its data on infrastructure it owns.

## Why intake is a good place to start

Intake is where a lot of admin piles up in a small firm. A potential client emails or calls. Someone collects their details, sometimes more than once. Information gets copied into a spreadsheet, a folder gets created, a conflict check gets run, an engagement letter goes out, and somebody has to remember to follow up.

None of it is hard. But it's repetitive, it's easy to drop, and it's time that never shows up on a bill. It's also the first impression a client has of your firm.

## The usual fix creates a new problem

The common answer is to buy an intake or practice management platform. Sometimes that's the right call. But often it means:

- **A new app everyone has to learn,** and that takes people out of how they already work.
- **Another place for information to live,** next to Outlook and your shared drive.
- **A vendor holding your client data,** which matters a lot in a profession built on confidentiality.

Law is generally slower than other industries to adopt new technology, often for good reasons. Asking a busy firm to move into a new platform is a big ask.

## Fit the technology to the firm instead

The approach I take with firms is the opposite: start with how the firm already works, and fit the technology around it.

With a small law firm I'm working with now, that means building intake directly into their existing tools. They work in Outlook and their files, so that's where the intake process lives. There's no new app to adopt and nothing pulling them out of their day.

In practice, that looks like:

1. **Capture once.** Details from the first contact are collected one time, in a consistent format, instead of being retyped.
2. **File it where it belongs.** A client folder is created and named the same way every time, with intake information saved inside it.
3. **Kick off the next step.** Reminders, follow-ups, and the routine back-and-forth are handled, so nothing depends on someone remembering.
4. **Keep it in the firm's hands.** The software runs on infrastructure the firm owns and controls, not on another vendor's servers.

## The goal is time back for clients

The point isn't technology for its own sake. It's that every hour your team spends retyping, filing, and chasing is an hour not spent with clients or on the work that matters. Fixing intake is usually the quickest way to give some of that time back.

Once intake is working, the same approach extends to the rest of the firm. If you're curious what that looks like, I wrote about [one search across Outlook, your files, and your case management software](/notes/law-firm-unified-search), and about [keeping your firm's data in your own hands](/notes/law-firm-own-your-data).

## If you only do one thing

Walk through your last five new clients and write down every step from first contact to opened matter, including who did it and where the information went. The steps that happen more than once, or depend on someone remembering, are where to start.
